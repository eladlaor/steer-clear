# Mainstream website blockers: features, pricing, scale, and business models (as of 2026-10-16)

## Who are the leading blockers, and what do they offer and charge?

### Takeaway
The market splits into (a) free or freemium Chrome extensions (BlockSite, StayFocusd, LeechBlock NG, StayFree and many lookalikes) and (b) paid cross-device or desktop apps (Freedom, Cold Turkey, Opal, RescueTime) that use a browser extension as one part of the product. Paid tiers sell **enforcement** (schedules, locked or strict modes, uninstall prevention, app blocking) and **cross-device sync**, not the act of blocking itself. LeechBlock NG, the closest free open-source peer, is at 100K users with a 4.9 rating and has no paid tier.

**LeechBlock NG**

No donation link appears on the listing. — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/blaaajhemilngeeffpbfkdjjoefldkok)

**Freedom (freedom.to)**

An account (email and password) is required, including on the free plan. Platforms: Mac, Windows, iOS, Android, Chromebook. — [freedom.to/pricing](https://freedom.to/pricing)

**Opal**
- iPhone, Android and desktop. Free basic tier. Paid: **$19.99/month or $99/year**, adding harder blocking difficulties, a focus score and reports. — [TechCrunch, 2025-12-25](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/25/the-best-distraction-blockers-to-jumpstart-your-focus-in-the-new-year)

**Forest**
- The browser extension is free. The iOS app is $3.99 one-time; the Android app is free with ads, or $1.99 to remove them. **one sec** (mobile "speed bump", the closest conceptual analogue to Steer Clear)

### Inferences
- In the Chrome store's top results, many entries are generic "Block Site" clones with high ratings. The blocking feature itself is commoditized and free.
- Steer Clear's distinct positioning (the user's own reason shown, a one-click alternative destination, an easy bypass, no account, local-only data, narrow per-site permissions) is unusual compared with the leaders. BlockSite discloses that it collects web history, and Freedom requires an account even for its free plan. LeechBlock's delay countdown and one sec's pause are the nearest feature analogues.
- The features the market charges for (Locked Mode, strict mode, uninstall prevention, scheduling) are mostly the *opposite* of Steer Clear's philosophy. Steer Clear's natural premium levers are therefore different ones (see the last section).

### Gaps
- No user counts or ratings could be fetched for StayFocusd, StayFree, Freedom's extension or the lookalike listings: the search page hides them and the StayFocusd page hit a redirect loop.
- No data was found on Plucky (its site did not resolve), Serene, SelfControl (free, open source, macOS; not verified this session) or Focus To-Do.
- BlockSite's current monthly and annual prices and free-tier cap were not confirmed from an official source in 2026.

## Which business models work in this category, and are there public revenue figures?

### Takeaway
Four models are visible: recurring subscription (Freedom, Opal, RescueTime, BlockSite), one-time lifetime license (Cold Turkey at $39, BlockSite's $39.99 lifetime offer, Freedom Forever at $99.50–$199), paid or ad-supported mobile apps (Forest), and free with donations (StayFocusd, LeechBlock). Almost all public revenue figures are third-party estimates, not company disclosures. The largest are about $10M ARR (annual recurring revenue) for Opal and about $150K/month for one sec.

### Cited Findings
- **Opal:** ARR.club estimates **$10M ARR** — [arr.club](https://www.arr.club/opal). Lazyweb estimates **$4.8M revenue in 2024**, 500K active users and about $6.9M raised — [Lazyweb](https://www.lazyweb.com/company/opal). Dealroom shows less than $1M (**stale**, valuation data from 2021) — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/opal-1-1/). Opal raised $4.3M in 2021 — [TechCrunch 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/26/opal-raises-4-3-million-for-its-digital-wellbeing-assistant-for-iphone/embed/). The sources conflict, and none cite company filings.
- **one sec:** A third-party estimate puts it at **$150K monthly revenue** with 95K downloads — [screensdesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/one-sec-screen-time-focus). The founder said revenue was "doubling every single month" after he started Instagram ads; early growth came from a viral tweet — [RevenueCat blog/podcast](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/frederik-riedel-expected-12-his-app-cut-screen-time-by-57).
- **Freedom:** No revenue disclosed. Its claim of 3M people is self-reported — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/@fstutzman). Freedom also sells team plans ("used by 3 million people for focused work") — same source.
- **BlockSite:** No revenue estimate found. The Chrome listing discloses web-history collection, so data-related revenue cannot be ruled out, though the listing claims no sale outside approved use cases — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/eiimnmioipafcokbfikbljfdeojpcgbh).
- **Ads:** Forest's Android app is ad-supported, with a $1.99 fee to remove ads — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/25/the-best-distraction-blockers-to-jumpstart-your-focus-in-the-new-year).
- **B2B and education:** Cold Turkey gives students 20% off — [getcoldturkey.com](https://getcoldturkey.com/pricing/). RescueTime sells Team plans — [CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/software/time-tracking/rescuetime/). Freedom markets team use — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/@fstutzman).

### Inferences
- The top revenue earners (Opal, one sec) are **mobile-first subscription apps** that grew through paid social ads and virality. Desktop or extension-only blockers show lower monetization, or are free.
- Lifetime licenses priced at roughly $39–$100 are common on desktop, which suggests a desktop audience tolerates one-time payments better than subscriptions.

### Gaps
- No verified revenue for Freedom, BlockSite, Cold Turkey or StayFocusd. Sensor Tower and Appfigures data were not accessible.

## What price anchors does a user see, and what is in a standard free tier versus premium?

### Takeaway
The anchors are about **$3–4/month billed annually (about $40/year)**, **$9–20/month billed monthly**, and **about $39–$100 for lifetime**. Free tiers typically allow basic or limited site blocking. Premium bundles scheduling, locked or strict modes, password and uninstall protection, category or keyword blocking, app blocking, sync and richer statistics.

### Cited Findings
- Freedom: $3.33/month yearly, $8.99/month monthly, $99.50–$199 lifetime; the free plan excludes scheduling and Locked Mode — [freedom.to/pricing](https://freedom.to/pricing).
- Opal: $19.99/month or $99/year — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/25/the-best-distraction-blockers-to-jumpstart-your-focus-in-the-new-year).
- Cold Turkey: free tier includes website blocking and statistics; the $39 lifetime Pro adds scheduling, locking and app blocking — [getcoldturkey.com](https://getcoldturkey.com/pricing/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/25/the-best-distraction-blockers-to-jumpstart-your-focus-in-the-new-year).
- BlockSite: $39.99 lifetime offer; premium covers category blocking, a custom block page and more; historical free cap of 6 sites (**stale**) — [blocksite.co/pricing](https://blocksite.co/pricing); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/blocksite/about/).
- RescueTime: about $6.50–12/month — [Jibble](https://www.jibble.io/reviews/rescuetime/rescuetime-pricing).

### Inferences
- A Steer Clear "Pro" priced above about $40/year or about $40 lifetime would sit above most anchors. For a gentle tool without lock enforcement, a lower price, or pay-what-you-want, is more plausible.

### Gaps
- No source states the conversion rate from free to paid for blockers.

## Can a free open-source tool like Steer Clear make money, and which open-source extensions do?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only modestly, and evidence of donations working is weak. The common pattern is a free open-source core plus a paid tier for sync or advanced features, sold through tooling such as ExtensionPay (5% per transaction). Donations alone rarely sustain a project, and the Chrome Web Store has no built-in support for developer donations.

### Cited Findings
- **ExtensionPay:** an open-source API built on Stripe for subscriptions, one-time payments and trials in extensions. It charges **5% per transaction** with no monthly fee, and says developers have earned **over $800K** through it. One testimonial reports nearly $4K in annual subscriptions in under a year. — [extensionpay.com](https://extensionpay.com/)
- ExtensionPay argues that donations are unsustainable because very few users give — [ExtensionPay article](https://extensionpay.com/articles/monetize-chrome-extensions-with-extensionpay). Another guide claims about 10% of users donate, with no data behind it (**unverified**) — [stefanvd.net](https://stefanvd.net/blog/?p=1500).
- Mozilla lists three routes: charging for features (freemium recommended), ads, and donations — [Mozilla Extension Workshop](https://extensionworkshop.com/documentation/publish/make-money-from-browser-extensions/).
- Chrome and Microsoft stores offer no features for supporting developers, and Mozilla moved its donation button below the fold — [armin.dev (2019, stale)](https://armin.dev/blog/2019/08/supporting-browser-extension-developers/).
- An Indie Hackers post titled "70,000 users on my Chrome extension and almost no revenue" illustrates that free users rarely convert by themselves (page returned 403; title only) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/70-000-users-on-my-chrome-extension-and-almost-no-revenue-a9495e2b39).
- Open-source peers: LeechBlock NG (100K users, free, no paid tier and no donation link on its listing) — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/blaaajhemilngeeffpbfkdjjoefldkok). StayFocusd relies on optional donations — [Kent](https://kent.ac.uk/software/stayfocusd).

### Inferences
- Realistic paths for Steer Clear: (1) a GitHub Sponsors or Ko-fi link plus a pay-what-you-want "supporter" unlock; (2) a Pro tier through ExtensionPay for opt-in features that fit its philosophy, such as cross-device sync (end-to-end encrypted, which would break the no-account promise only for people who opt in), richer reflection or statistics exports, or Firefox/Edge/Safari builds; (3) team or education licensing. Steer Clear's privacy-first, local-only stance is a differentiator against BlockSite's history collection and Freedom's mandatory account, and should not be traded away for ad or data revenue.
- Category revenue leaders are mobile apps, so a meaningful revenue ceiling probably requires a mobile companion app rather than a Chrome extension alone.

### Gaps
- No verified revenue figures were found for open-source productivity extensions that use donations or open-core models; ExtensionPay's "$45k/month" teaser was not substantiated.
