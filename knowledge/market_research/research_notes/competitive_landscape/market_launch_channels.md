# Market Size and Launch Channels for a Small Free Focus Extension

Research date: 2026-10-16. All analyst market figures are vendor estimates from paid-report publishers. Their definitions differ, and the reports disagree widely.

## How big is the digital-wellbeing / screen-time / focus market, and how fast is it growing?

### Takeaway
No analyst firm sizes "digital wellbeing / focus tools" as a single segment. The adjacent estimates range from about $1B for "digital detox apps" to $2–4B for "screen time management software", with growth estimates of 7–24% a year. The better demand proxy is user counts: the largest browser blockers each have around 1M Chrome users, and the leading paid apps report 1–3M+ users.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- For positioning, cite the narrow range ("$1–4B by 2025 vendor estimates for screen-time/detox software") together with the caveat, and lean on the user-count proxy instead. Free extensions like Unhook and News Feed Eradicator reached 200K–1M users without paid marketing. That is credible evidence of demand for free, single-purpose, no-account tools.
- The soft-friction / intention-prompt niche (Intention at about 6K users with a near-perfect rating; one sec on mobile) is under-served on desktop. Intention's Firefox build looks stale (last updated 2021), which leaves room for Steer Clear.

### Gaps
- Sensor Tower and data.ai figures for the category were not found in public sources. Those are paywalled.
- The Grand View Research figure specifically for "digital wellbeing" was not retrieved.
- The one sec PNAS 2023 field study (Grüning, Riedel, Lorenz-Spreen) could not be verified through search. It is worth citing if confirmed directly on pnas.org, since it is the strongest academic support for friction-based interventions.

## How does Chrome Web Store discovery work in 2026, and how crowded is Productivity?

### Takeaway
Store search ranks by listing metadata plus a heuristic of ratings and usage, including install-to-uninstall trends. The Featured badge comes from manual review, and developers can nominate their own extension for it. Steer Clear meets every published nomination criterion. Productivity is the most crowded category: about 62K of about 112K extensions, and most extensions have fewer than 1,000 users.

### Cited Findings
- Search ranking uses "a number of criteria, including metadata from your item's listing page". The ranking heuristic considers "ratings from users as well as usage statistics", for example download-to-uninstall trends over time. Google does not publish the weights — [Chrome for Developers: Discovery](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/discovery)
- Featured badge: Chrome team members evaluate extensions manually, looking at adherence to best practices and at listing quality ("quality images and a detailed description"). There is no checklist that guarantees the badge, and developers cannot pay for it — [Chrome for Developers: Discovery](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/discovery); [Google blog, 2022 badge launch](https://blog.google/products/chrome/find-great-extensions-new-chrome-web-store-badges/)
- Featured nomination is done through the One Stop Support page and is described as a trial. To qualify, the item must be an extension you own, have English support, be published and public, have no active policy violations, and have "core features ... accessible without additional credentials or payments" — [Chrome for Developers: Discovery](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/discovery)
- Featured extensions "may receive higher rankings in search and filtering" and may appear in promotions on and off the store — [Chrome for Developers: Discovery](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/discovery)
- Established Publisher badge: granted automatically to verified publishers with a consistent positive track record. New developers need at least a few months of compliance. The badge covers nearly 75% of extensions — [Chrome for Developers: Discovery](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/discovery)
- Editorial collections are curated, and "solicitations to be placed in a collection are not accepted". Promoting your listing independently can help — [Chrome for Developers: Discovery](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/discovery)
- Store size: DebugBear counted 111,933 extensions, of which about 62K (about 55%) were in Productivity. Only 242 extensions (0.2%) have 1M+ users, and 86.3% have fewer than 1,000 users. The data is from 2024 — [DebugBear](https://www.debugbear.com/blog/chrome-extension-statistics). Other secondary sites claim 179K or 250K+ extensions in 2025. Those totals conflict and are not authoritative — [aboutchromebooks](https://www.aboutchromebooks.com/chrome-extension-ecosystem/)
- Both of the largest free comparables (Unhook, News Feed Eradicator) carry the Featured badge (CWS listings above). Both have thousands of ratings.

### Inferences
- Nominating Steer Clear for the Featured badge should be an early launch action. It is free, no-account, MIT-licensed and MV3, so it meets all of the stated criteria. Polish the listing first (screenshots, a promo video, a detailed description), because review focuses on the listing.
- Because uninstall trends feed the ranking, first-week retention (onboarding that gets the user to "the first moment a block saves you") matters as much as install volume.
- Ratings are a ranking input, and every comparable at scale has 2K–9K ratings. A gentle in-extension rating prompt after a few successful "steers" is worth adding.
- "Productivity" has about 62K competitors. Unhook sits in "Workflow & Planning" and News Feed Eradicator in "Tools", so Steer Clear's category choice is worth testing.

### Gaps
- Google publishes no rating-count thresholds or keyword weightings, and I found no reliable study of how much rating count moves ranking.
- The 2026 total extension count in the Productivity category was not available from an official source. The DebugBear 2024 data is the best available.
- The claim that MV2 extensions lost Featured status after June 2024 comes from a third-party blog and was not verified.

## How did comparable tools get their first users? (Product Hunt, Show HN, Reddit, press, creators)

### Takeaway
Show HN is close to useless for blockers: dozens of blocker launches from 2018–2026 scored 1–4 points, and the best scored 17–24. Product Hunt can give a one-day bump (#2 of the day is the best documented result for a blocker), but indie installs from it are small. The tools that reached scale did it through (a) store organic search plus the Featured badge over years (Unhook, News Feed Eradicator), (b) press and app-store editorial (one sec), or (c) creator-led short video (Opal). Indie-hacker anecdotes put realistic early numbers at hundreds of installs in the first months.

### Cited Findings
**Hacker News (HN Algolia API, queried directly):**
- "Show HN" blocker launches mostly score 1–4 points, for example: Site Blocker (2025, 2 pts), DecayBlock (2025-10, 4 pts), YouTube Shorts Blocker (2025, 3 pts), QuietHours (2026-05, 1 pt), and PIM, "app blocker that inconveniences you instead of locking you out" (2026-06, 3 pts; conceptually close to Steer Clear). Exceptions: Chomper, a Linux blocker (2018, 24 pts / 12 comments), and BlockTube (2018, 17 pts / 16 comments), both open-source GitHub-linked launches — [HN Algolia query](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=Show%20HN%20blocker&tags=show_hn&hitsPerPage=40)
- The 2015 "Show HN: News Feed Eradicator" got 1 point and 0 comments. Despite that, the extension now has 200K Chrome users — [HN Algolia query](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=News%20Feed%20Eradicator&tags=story&hitsPerPage=20)
- News Feed Eradicator was created by Jordan West in 2012 to fight his own procrastination — [NoypiGeeks](https://www.noypigeeks.com/how-tos/news-feed-eradicator/)

**Product Hunt:**
- Just Focus (a simple Chrome website blocker, 2018): 214 upvotes, 29 comments, #2 Product of the Day (third-party tracker) — [hunted.space](https://hunted.space/dashboard/138529); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/just-focus-2)
- Focus Dude (2021): #20 on the day — [hunted.space](https://hunted.space/dashboard/303685)
- An indie founder's first Chrome extension got 4 upvotes on Product Hunt — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/shipped-my-first-chrome-extension-launched-on-product-hunt-2-days-ago-got-4-upvotes-heres-what-i-learned-b08dc828a2). Another reported 33 installs, all organic, from a Product Hunt launch — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-got-my-first-150-users-on-my-chrome-extension-a4046fd106)
- No Product Hunt launch records were found for Intention, Opal, Unhook or one sec.

**Creator / short video (Opal):**
- Opal hired a TikTok user who was already mentioning the app. The resulting "Olivia Unplugged" channel teaches screen-time and focus content and reached about 700K followers across platforms in a few months. Opal also licensed an organic creator video that reached tens of millions and ran it as a paid ad — [BetterLaunch](https://www.betterlaunch.co/playbooks/kenneth-schlenker-opal-billion-user-9)
- The brand-owned creator account has "no overt product messaging": the app appears inside storylines, and the bio says "Powered by Opal". It drew 8M views in 30 days — [Milk Karten](https://www.milkkarten.net/p/creator-brand-olivia-unplugged-opal)
- An Opal job post says the organic creator channel grew from 0 to 300K followers in 3 months, targeting US women aged 25–35 — per search summary of [euremotejobs](https://euremotejobs.com/?p=45089)

**Press / platform editorial (one sec):**
- one sec's founder won an award in Apple's App Store Foundation Program, and the app was covered by German tech press ("saves 2,000 years of life") — [NextPit](https://nextpit.com/german-developer-saves-2000-years-life-with-app). It was also featured in roundup coverage of "apps to help you disconnect" — [SAN](https://san.com/cc/everyone-is-glued-to-their-phones-these-apps-and-gadgets-may-help-you-disconnect/)

**Indie Hackers / Reddit anecdotes:**
- One focus-tool founder had 290 installs in 4 months solo. Their key lesson: a blocker only feels valuable at the moment it saves you, not at setup — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/im-a-3rd-year-cs-student-who-built-a-chrome-extension-in-a-week-here-s-what-i-learned-trying-to-get-my-first-real-users-346b25f671)
- Reddit karma gates block new accounts from posting in large subreddits, so build karma before the launch post. Users search for problems, not product names, so listing SEO ("search engine optimization") matters — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-got-my-first-150-users-on-my-chrome-extension-a4046fd106)
- Roamer got at least 50 installs from a partner indie's Product Hunt launch (cross-promotion) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/roamer)

### Inferences
- Do a Show HN, but frame it around the open-source code and the design idea ("a blocker that shows you your own reason instead of a wall"), not "yet another blocker". Expect little, and treat any comment thread as feedback. GitHub-linked, open-source framing correlates with the few blocker posts that scored above single digits.
- Product Hunt is worth one coordinated day. Realistic outcomes range from tens to a few hundred installs; the main value is backlinks and a "Featured on PH" social-proof badge.
- The highest-leverage repeatable channel for a solo dev is probably short-form "here is how I stopped doomscrolling" content (TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts) plus outreach to existing focus/ADHD creators. The Opal case shows that narrative-first, product-second content works in this category.
- Long-term growth for the free comparables came from store search plus the Featured badge compounding over years, not from launch day.

### Gaps
- No primary launch post-mortems were found for Unhook, Cold Turkey, Freedom, Minimalist Phone or Intention. Their first-user stories remain unknown.
- No Reddit launch threads with measurable results were retrieved (Reddit search was not directly queried).

## Which positioning phrases get traction now: digital minimalism, dopamine detox, ADHD, or intentional internet use?

### Takeaway
All four themes are rising. "Brain rot" and "doomscrolling" are the mainstream vernacular. "Dopamine detox" is popular but is being publicly debunked by clinicians. "Intentional use" and "digital minimalism" are the research-grounded framings, and they match Steer Clear's soft-friction design. No hard side-by-side search-volume data was found.

### Cited Findings
- Searches for "dopamine detox", "screen time addiction" and "digital minimalism" have reportedly risen steadily over five years, especially in the UK and US. UK "digital detox" searches more than tripled in early 2024. This is a secondary source with no underlying figures — per search summary of [Get Lost HQ](https://getlosthq.substack.com/p/beyond-the-endless-scroll)
- Clinicians push back on "dopamine detox": avoiding stimulation does not lower dopamine or "clean" the brain (Harvard Health, Cleveland Clinic, as cited) — per search summary of [El Español, 2026-05](https://www.elespanol.com/ciencia/salud/20260525/psicologos-coinciden-dejar-consumir-pantallas-golpe-no-funciona-recuperar-atencion/1003744255714_0.amp.html). The same article reports that psychologists agree quitting screens abruptly doesn't work for recovering attention, which supports gradual, soft friction.
- "Brain rot" was Oxford's 2024 Word of the Year, and Gen Z / Gen Alpha use it widely for endless scrolling — [web.de](https://web.de/magazine/wissen/psychologie/oxford-wort-jahres-2024-bedeutet-brain-rot-40471364); [Psi Chi](https://www.psichi.org/blogpost/987366/515055/Brain-Rot-Generational-Fad-or-Generational-Problem-What-Is-Brain-Rot)
- Cold Turkey Blocker shows as a trending search on Exploding Topics (about 10–12K monthly volume, with large growth in some windows) — [Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/topic/cold-turkey-blocker?period=24)
- Recent indie blocker launches on HN used "doomscrolling" (Site Blocker, Doomscroll Blocker) and "inconveniences you instead of locking you out" (PIM) framing — [HN Algolia query](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=Show%20HN%20blocker&tags=show_hn&hitsPerPage=40)
- Intention's tagline is "Stop Mindless Browsing" — [CWS listing](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/dladanhaondcgpahgiflodhckhoeohoe)

### Inferences
- Recommended: lead with plain-language problem phrases users actually search ("stop doomscrolling", "block distracting sites", "mindless browsing"). Then differentiate on the "soft" mechanism ("a nudge, not a wall"; "your own reason, one click to something better"). That avoids competing head-on with hard blockers like Cold Turkey.
- Avoid the "dopamine detox" claim in the listing. It is popular, but it is scientifically contested and could invite criticism. Use it only as a content/SEO hook in social posts, if at all.
- ADHD framing has strong creator communities, but it carries health-claim risk. Phrase it as "built for people who get pulled off-task", not as treatment.
- "Intentional internet use" matches the product exactly and has research backing (self-nudging), but it is lower-volume as a search term. Use it as the tagline and philosophy, not as the primary keyword.

### Gaps
- No Google Trends side-by-side for these terms was retrieved; that would need a direct Trends check.
- No data was found on ADHD-specific search volume for focus tools.

## How much reach do Firefox, Edge and Safari ports add?

### Takeaway
On desktop, Chrome is about 65–72% of the worldwide market. Edge adds about 10–15%, Firefox about 6% (about 14% in the US), and Safari about 5–8%. An Edge port is nearly free, since it accepts the same MV3 package, and Edge users can also install from the Chrome store. A Firefox port reaches a privacy-minded, open-source-friendly audience that over-indexes for this category. Safari requires Xcode packaging and an Apple developer account, and adds the least for the effort.

### Cited Findings
- Worldwide desktop browser share, June 2026: Chrome 72.24%, Edge 10.45%, Firefox 6.31%, Safari 5.18%, Opera 1.81% — [StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/browser-market-share/desktop)
- Worldwide desktop + tablet/console, September 2026: Chrome 65.09%, Edge 14.64%, Safari 8.06%, Firefox 6.11% — [StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/browser-market-share/desktop-tabletconsole/worldwide)
- US desktop, June 2026: Chrome 59.66%, Firefox 13.96%, Edge 12.24%, Safari 9.02% — [StatCounter US](https://gs.statcounter.com/browser-market-share/desktop/united-states-of-america)
- Comparable cross-store ratios: Unhook has about 1M users on Chrome vs 100K+ on Edge (about 10%) — [CWS](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/unhook-remove-youtube-rec/khncfooichmfjbepaaaebmommgaepoid), [Edge](https://microsoftedge.microsoft.com/addons/detail/unhook-remove-youtube-r/hebpjnnclppdnfghdnmhgdljmjpfhggk). BlockSite has 1M on Chrome vs about 32–35K on Firefox (about 3%) — [CWS](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/eiimnmioipafcokbfikbljfdeojpcgbh), [AMO](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/3145). LeechBlock NG, an open-source tool born on Firefox, has about 113K Firefox users — [AMO](https://addons.mozilla.org/addon/leechblock-ng)

### Inferences
- Port priority: Edge first (minimal effort; about 10% more reach, consistent with Unhook's ratio). Firefox second: lower share, but its open-source/privacy audience fits an MIT-licensed, local-only tool, and the AMO store (addons.mozilla.org, "A-M-O") is far less crowded than Chrome's. Safari last.
- Expect ports to add roughly 10–20% to total installs, not to multiply them. Their bigger value may be credibility ("works everywhere") in launch posts.

### Gaps
- No direct data was found on Safari extension install volumes for comparable tools.
- The claim that Firefox users over-index for privacy/OSS tools is inferred from LeechBlock's Firefox-first success, not measured.
