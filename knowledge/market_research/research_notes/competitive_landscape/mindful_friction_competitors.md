# Mindful-friction competitors of Steer Clear (as of 2026-10-16)

## Which products occupy the mindful-friction niche (pause / interstitial / intention prompt rather than hard blocking)?

### Takeaway
The niche has one category leader that spans browser and mobile (one sec, about 40K Chrome users, a paid "Pro" plan), one strong browser-only incumbent (Intention, 7K users, last updated March 2025), Freedom's free "Pause" (10K users, not updated since 2023), and a long tail of tiny 2025–2026 Chrome extensions (PauseTab, ZenBlock, pause.do, Mindful Redirect, speedbump, Pause & Breathe and others), most with fewer than 500 users. On mobile, ScreenZen (free, tips only) and one sec lead. Opal is mainly a hard blocker and has abandoned Chrome. HabitLab is effectively dormant.

### Cited Findings

**one sec (riedel.wtf GmbH, Germany): browser extension plus iOS, Android and Mac**
- Chrome listing: 4.9 stars from 1.2K ratings, about 40,000 users, v1.4.2, updated Sept 25, 2026, offers in-app purchases. The developer says usage data is stored offline on the device. The listing does not list permissions on the page. — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/femnahohginddofgekknfmaklcbpinkn)
- Extension: "mindful pause before you can open distracting websites", with Re-Intervention, Blocking and customizable Interventions. Supports Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge. The Mac app can also intervene on desktop apps. — [one-sec.app/platforms](https://one-sec.app/platforms/)
- Pro pricing: €3.99/month, €14.99/year, €99.99 lifetime (varies by region), with a 1-week trial. The free version covers 1 app with a breathing interruption. Intervention types include breathing, intention or emotion tracking, and "healthy alternatives" reminders (for example, read a book or call a friend). It also offers progress diagrams and time-saved stats. An account system syncs Pro across devices. — [one sec FAQ](https://one-sec.app/faq/)
- US App Store pricing per a third party (checked Oct 10, 2026): $6.99/month, $19.99/year, $99.99 lifetime; 23,675 ratings (4.8). The bypass is "tap yes each time to proceed." — [Habit Doom comparison](https://habitdoom.com/blog/opal-vs-screenzen-jomo-one-sec)
- one sec's site cites a Max Planck study claiming a 57% average drop in app usage. A competitor page repeats it, and I could not verify it independently. — [getdull.app](https://getdull.app/one-sec-alternative/)

**Intention – Stop Mindless Browsing (Road to Ramen LLC, getintention.com): Chrome and Firefox**
- Chrome: 7,000 users, 4.8 stars from 1K ratings, v1.5.0, last updated March 15, 2025, "Featured" badge, developer says no data collected. — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/dladanhaondcgpahgiflodhckhoeohoe)
- Mechanism: on a distracting site, the user must choose how long to unlock it (a time-limit commitment). It shows the remaining time, auto-ends the session and prompts reflection. Also offers a daily limit across all distracting sites and a streak counter. No redirect is described, and no pricing is shown. — [getintention.com](https://www.getintention.com/); [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/dladanhaondcgpahgiflodhckhoeohoe)
- Firefox: 4.9 stars from 658 reviews. — [AMO](https://addons.mozilla.org/firefox/addon/intention/)

**Pause – Stop Mindless Browsing (Freedom Labs / Eighty Percent Solutions)**
- About 10,000 users, 4.4 stars from 124 ratings, v1.0.1, last updated Oct 3, 2023 (stale). A green "calming" screen appears for 5 seconds (adjustable), then you can continue, stay or close. Ships with 50 preset sites. Developer says no data leaves the device. No stats, redirect or pricing are mentioned. — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/ljfdccdjpfjpfjbpdiihanpodilolofh)

**With Intention (Alex Widua): open source**
- 328 users, 4.5 stars from 11 ratings, v0.2, last updated Oct 21, 2021 (abandoned). Asks the user to write a stated intention on each visit. GitHub repo linked from the listing. — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fccbnilmkfoeamilfnaannjjocnnigio)

**PauseTab (David Hook, UK): new freemium entrant**
- v0.1.0, updated April 1, 2026, 0 ratings, user count not shown. Free tier: up to 3 sites, delay timer, optional intention prompt, daily counters, popup stats, presets, import/export. Pro tier: unlimited rules, schedules, budgets, session caps, temporary passes, weekly summaries and sync, sold through its website via Stripe and activated with an account code. The store privacy disclosure lists "Personally identifiable information" as handled. Pro price not found. — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/enghckakgjjoncdcdlkagoodggpgoleg)

**ZenBlock: Mindful Web Blocker & Focus Timer (zenblock.app)**
- 125 users, 4.6 stars from 9 ratings, v1.0.9, updated Feb 18, 2026. A 30-second breathing "Mindful Pause" before an Unlock button. Strict Mode hides Unlock and adds a password. Analytics compare "Mindful Breaths" with unlocks. Also offers Pomodoro and scheduled blocking. In-app purchases, no prices shown. Requests "Read and change all your data on the websites you visit." The disclosure lists PII as handled. — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/gnjnoncblhhofbhekoogfohpppkbiclo)

**Mindful Redirect (GitHub yeetric/self-control-mindfulness): the closest feature match**
- 27 users, v1.0.1, updated May 26, 2026. Redirects listed sites to an intermediate page where the user can exit, go to a redirect site, or access the site. Access requires journaling, a mindfulness routine, or a countdown. Offers custom messages, unblock expiration and a configurable redirect target. — [ChromeBoard mirror](https://chromeboard.com/extension/mindful-redirect-eaamioafeknhkapchlceggfdcombeoal) (third-party mirror, not the store itself)

**pause.do**
- A "privacy-first" browser extension with pause types Think First, Scroll Pause, Session Nudge, Focus Limit and Tab Overload. It also targets AI prompting. Launched on Product Hunt March 23, 2026 (#7 of the day, 140 points). No pricing listed. — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/pause-do-don-t-outsource-your-thinking/launches/pause-do)

**Mobile mindful-friction apps (not browser competitors, but they set user expectations)**
- ScreenZen: free with optional $5–$40 tips. A pause before an app opens, an "Is this important?" prompt, a count of opens left, daily limits, cooldowns, optional strict block. No account needed. iPhone, Android and Mac. 51,312 US App Store ratings (4.9). — [Habit Doom](https://habitdoom.com/blog/opal-vs-screenzen-jomo-one-sec); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1541027222)
- ScreenZen Blocker for Firefox exists only as a companion to the macOS app. I found no Chrome extension. — [AMO](https://addons.mozilla.org/addon/screenzen-blocker/)
- Opal: primarily hard blocking (schedules, limits, Hard Mode), $19.99/month, $99.99/year, $399 lifetime, free plan of 1 rule, account required. — [Habit Doom](https://habitdoom.com/blog/opal-vs-screenzen-jomo-one-sec). Its Chrome extension was deprecated ("please use Mac app instead," Nov 2024). — [Opal community](https://community.opal.so/t/chrome-extension-depreciated-please-use-mac-app-instead/6271). - Jomo: scheduled blocking, $5.99/month, $29.99/year, $99.99 lifetime, iPhone and Mac. — [Habit Doom](https://habitdoom.com/blog/opal-vs-screenzen-jomo-one-sec)
- Open-source Android options: Mindful (GPL-2.0), Reef Wellbeing (MIT), ReDD Focus, QuestPhone. — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/one-sec/)

**HabitLab (Stanford HCI)**
- Research extension that rotated interventions. Over 12,000 daily active users in 2019. — [Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/2019/03/13/habitlab-browser-extension-aims-to-help-users-regain-control-of-their-online-browsing-behavior/). **Feed removers (adjacent, not friction)**
- News Feed Eradicator hides the Facebook feed and replaces it with a quote. Available for Chrome and Firefox. — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/how-to/how-to-hide-your-facebook-news-feed)

**Built-in browser features**
- Chrome on Android with Digital Wellbeing has had per-site timers since 2019. When time runs out, the site is "paused" until the next day, and Incognito is excluded. That is a hard daily stop, not a friction prompt. — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2019/05/20/digital-wellbeing-for-websites/); [Vivaldi forum](https://forum.vivaldi.net/topic/80349/digital-wellbeing-site-timers)

### Inferences
- **The scale picture is lopsided.** one sec (about 40K Chrome users, active, cross-platform, paid) is the only well-funded browser-side player. Intention (7K) is the only other well-rated, sizable browser-only product, and it hasn't shipped since March 2025. Freedom's Pause (10K) has been stale for 3 years. Everything launched in 2025–2026 has fewer than about 500 users. A small open-source entrant can credibly reach the "#3–4 browser-side" tier.
- **The dominant interruption content is generic:** breathing (one sec, ZenBlock, Pause & Breathe, Zen Pause), a timer or colour screen (Freedom Pause), a time-commitment unlock (Intention), or a fresh typed intention (With Intention, PauseTab, one sec's intention mode). Showing the user's own previously written reason is rare. The only near-matches found are Block Reddit's user-added "goal or task" (Reddit only) and Mindful Redirect's "custom messages."
- **A one-click alternative destination inside the pause page is rare.** Redirect exists either as a forced redirect (BlockSite, Alternate Redirector, Mindful Redirect) or as a text suggestion (one sec's "healthy alternatives" reminder). Only Mindful Redirect (27 users) combines a pause page with a "redirect" button. No major product found does.
- From prior product knowledge, not re-verified this session: LeechBlock can show a delaying page and a custom block-page URL, which partly overlaps the redirect feature, but it is configuration-heavy.

### Gaps
- Chrome Web Store pages for HabitLab, Unhook, News Feed Eradicator (main listing) and the "Mindful Internet Use" ID (hieolpjdilnibgamiafklnlcmagdngoo) did not render ("empty-title"), so their current user counts and update dates are unverified. "Mindful Internet Use" may have been removed.
- I found no product called "Mindful Browsing" by that exact name.
- Exact pricing for PauseTab Pro, ZenBlock IAP, pause.do and Intention (likely free) not found.
- No Brave or Edge built-in mindful-friction feature found. None was searched in depth beyond Chrome Android Digital Wellbeing.
- Reddit (r/nosurf, r/digitalminimalism) sentiment was not captured. The search returned only Reddit-blocker add-ons.

## Which of Steer Clear's features are rare vs common in this niche?

### Takeaway
Steer Clear's distinctive combination is a reminder showing the user's own pre-written reason, plus a one-click jump to a chosen alternative, plus a deliberately easy 5-minute bypass, plus open source with no account and per-site permissions. Each piece exists somewhere, but I found no product with more than about 100 users that combines the first two. Steer Clear is at parity on the pause itself, the timed bypass and local stats. It is behind on cross-platform reach, mobile, breathing or intention variety, social proof, and ratings.

### Cited Findings
- **User's own previously written reason: rare.** The near-matches are Block Reddit's user-added goal or task (Reddit only) and Mindful Redirect's custom messages (27 users). Others ask for a fresh intention each time (With Intention, PauseTab, one sec) or show breathing or timers. — [Block Reddit](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/block-reddit/dghpifbabagfbmekemjgccimackjiljp/reviews); [Mindful Redirect](https://chromeboard.com/extension/mindful-redirect-eaamioafeknhkapchlceggfdcombeoal); [one sec FAQ](https://one-sec.app/faq/)
- **One-click alternative destination: rare inside a pause.** It is common only as a forced redirect in blockers (BlockSite per-site, Alternate Redirector, Elseware). one sec suggests alternatives as text. — [TechRadar BlockSite](https://www.techradar.com/reviews/blocksite); [one sec FAQ](https://one-sec.app/faq/)
- **Easy timed bypass: common.** Intention has a user-chosen unlock window, PauseTab Pro has temporary passes, Reddit Focus has a 10-minute pass, Mindful Redirect has unblock expiry, and Freedom Pause continues after 5 seconds. — sources as above
- **Local-only stats: moderately common** (one sec, ZenBlock, PauseTab, Intention streaks, Pause & Breathe tally). Hour-of-day and day-of-week breakdowns were not seen in any listing. — sources as above
- **Open source and no account: uncommon among browser pause extensions.** Only With Intention (abandoned 2021), Mindful Redirect (27 users) and HabitLab (dormant) were found open source. Paid competitors tie Pro to accounts or account codes (one sec, PauseTab, Opal). — sources as above
- **Free with no feature cap:** one sec free covers 1 app or site, PauseTab free covers 3 sites, and BlockSite reportedly caps free at 6. ScreenZen (mobile) and Intention are the main fully free incumbents. — [one sec FAQ](https://one-sec.app/faq/); [PauseTab](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/enghckakgjjoncdcdlkagoodggpgoleg); [Habit Doom](https://habitdoom.com/blog/opal-vs-screenzen-jomo-one-sec)

### Inferences
- **Unique (as far as found):** the stored personal reason combined with a one-click alternative button on the same interstitial; per-site optional host permissions as a privacy stance; hour-of-day and day-of-week stats with one-click erase.
- **Parity:** the pause interstitial, timed bypass, auto-continue countdown (similar to Freedom Pause and PauseTab), local stats, free.
- **Behind:** no mobile or Safari/Firefox reach (one sec, Intention); no breathing or exercise variety; no daily budget or streak (Intention, PauseTab); no strict mode for users who want escalation (ZenBlock, ScreenZen); zero reviews versus one sec's 1.2K and Intention's 1K.
- **Positioning opportunity:** Intention's 7K users with no update in 19 months, and Freedom Pause's 3-year staleness, suggest an underserved, free, browser-only audience. one sec's free tier (1 site) pushes power users toward a paid plan, which a fully free, unlimited, open-source tool undercuts.

### Gaps
- No Reddit or user-interview evidence was gathered on whether users value "my own reason" over breathing or intention prompts. This is a demand hypothesis, not a validated finding.
- I could not confirm whether one sec's custom interventions can display a user-written static message, which would weaken the "own reason" uniqueness claim. Check one sec's tutorials at tutorials.one-sec.app.
